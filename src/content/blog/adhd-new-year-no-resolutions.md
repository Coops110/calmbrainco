January arrives with the same offer every year: pick a resolution, keep it every single day, feel like a failure the first time you don't. For a lot of ADHD brains, that offer was rigged from the start, and it's worth saying plainly why.

## Why don't traditional resolutions work for ADHD brains

Resolutions quietly assume a few things: sustained motivation over months, consistency without needing novelty to stay interested, and the ability to tolerate a delayed reward. An ADHD brain is often wired closer to the opposite of all three — it runs on interest and quick feedback, not scheduled willpower. A vague resolution like "get more organized" doesn't give it anything to actually grab onto.

## What happens after the first missed day

For most streak-based systems, a missed day doesn't stay a missed day — it becomes the reason the whole goal gets dropped. The guilt from breaking a chain is often bigger than the cost of the single missed day itself, and the guilt is what actually kills the goal, not the original slip.

## What's a re-entry point, and why does it work better

A re-entry point is a rule, written down before you need it, for exactly what happens after a miss: "if I miss a day, I take a 5-minute version instead of none. If I miss a whole week, I restart next Monday, no explanation needed." Deciding this in advance, while it's easy and abstract, means there's nothing to negotiate with yourself in the moment a slip actually happens.

## Does this mean no real goal-setting at all

No — it means specific, small, and flexible instead of vague and rigid. "Save an extra $50 a paycheck" or "walk three times a week, five minutes counts on a bad day" give a brain something concrete to check against, without the all-or-nothing trap a bigger, vaguer goal sets up.

## What should a monthly check-in look like instead of a streak

One honest question a month, not thirty check marks: did I move toward this at all? A blank or "no" month isn't a failure entry, it's just information — the system is built to keep going through it, not to get broken by it.
