A spreadsheet is a perfectly good tool for tracking money, right up until the moment you forget it exists. That's not a discipline problem — it's what happens when a system depends entirely on remembering to open it, with no physical trigger and no visible reminder sitting in the room with you.

## The real failure point isn't math, it's memory

Most budgeting advice assumes the hard part is the arithmetic: categorizing a purchase, running a total, checking it against a limit. For an ADHD brain, the arithmetic was never the problem. The problem is that a spreadsheet lives behind a login, on a device, in a tab that gets closed — and once it's out of sight, it's out of mind until a bank notification says something's gone wrong.

## Paper stays visible in a way a file never does

A printed page sitting on a desk or tucked into a planner doesn't need to be opened, loaded, or remembered as a separate app. It's just there. That's the entire advantage — not that paper is more powerful than software, but that it doesn't depend on a habit that ADHD brains are specifically bad at sustaining: checking in on something nobody reminded you to check.
