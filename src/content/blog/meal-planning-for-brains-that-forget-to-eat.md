Skipping a meal sounds like a choice. For a lot of ADHD brains, it isn't — hyperfocus swallows hours without warning, and hunger cues that should interrupt it just don't land until they're impossible to ignore.

## Traditional meal plans assume a consistent week

Most meal-planning templates are built around a week that looks the same every day: same wake time, same meal windows, same energy to cook. An ADHD week rarely looks like that. A rigid seven-day grid with a recipe locked into every slot just becomes another thing to fall behind on, and falling behind on a meal plan feels worse than never having one.

## Plan loosely, not perfectly

A meal planner that actually gets used plans loosely — a short list of realistic options for the week, not a locked schedule — so there's still a plan on a day that goes sideways. The goal isn't a perfectly planned week. It's making sure "what's for dinner" has an easy answer even on a day where cooking from scratch was never going to happen.
