Skipping a meal sounds like a choice. For many ADHD brains, it is not: hyperfocus swallows hours without warning, and hunger cues that should interrupt it do not land until they are hard to ignore.

## Why traditional meal plans fail

Most meal-planning templates are built around a week that looks the same every day: same wake time, same meal windows, same energy to cook. An ADHD week rarely looks like that. A rigid seven-day grid with a recipe locked into every slot becomes another thing to fall behind on, and falling behind on a meal plan feels worse than never having one.

## What a looser meal plan looks like

Plan loosely: a short list of realistic options for the week rather than a locked schedule, so there is still a plan on a day that goes sideways. The goal is not a perfectly planned week. It is making sure "what's for dinner" has an easy answer even on a day where cooking from scratch was never going to happen.

## How to plan meals in ten minutes a week

Write down three to five meals you can actually make this week, not an ideal week's worth. Add a default easy dinner for the days nothing else happens. Keep a running grocery list beside it so deciding happens once.

## What if you forget to eat during the day

Some people find pairing meals with an existing routine, or a recurring alarm, helps keep food on the radar during a hyperfocus stretch. If skipped meals are affecting your health, that is a conversation for a clinician, not a planner.
