A task sits on the list. You know exactly what it is and exactly how to do it. You still haven't started it, and an hour has gone by. That is not a motivation problem. It is what happens when the hard part of a task isn't the doing, it's the starting, and no amount of knowing what to do fixes a stall at the starting line.

## Is the problem laziness

No. "Just start" treats the gap between knowing a task and beginning it as a willpower shortfall. For a lot of ADHD brains, that gap is a real cognitive step called task initiation, and it is specifically harder to clear than the task itself. A report that takes forty minutes to write can take two hours to start, and finishing the report in forty minutes once it's underway is proof the skill was never missing.

## Why doesn't a big task list help

A list that says "finish the report" names the outcome, not a starting point. The brain doing the stalling isn't missing information about what "finish the report" means. It's missing something small enough to begin on immediately, with no decision attached. A task that stays sized at "finish the report" stays unstartable for exactly the same reason all day.

## What actually gets a stalled task moving

Breaking the task down until the next piece is small enough to start in the next two minutes, with no judgment call left to make about where to begin. "Open the report and write one sentence" starts. "Finish the report" doesn't, no matter how many times it gets re-read.

## Does this mean big projects get broken into tiny pieces forever

No, just the starting edge. Once a task is moving, momentum tends to carry it further than the first tiny step alone. The breakdown is only there to clear the stall, not to turn every task into a checklist of two-minute pieces.
