Most student planners assume a brain that starts an assignment the week it's handed out and works on it a little every day. A lot of ADHD brains don't work that way, and treating that as a discipline failure instead of just how the brain runs is where most school planners break. The ADHD Student Planner is built around the brain that's actually doing the work, including the one that does its best work the night before, on purpose.

Ten pages: a class schedule and contacts page so instructors and office hours live in one place, an assignment and project tracker written as "due in ___ days" instead of a calendar date so the page never goes out of date, a big project breakdown that reuses the brand's mountain-to-pebble mechanism, a study session page with a body-doubling slot and a distraction parking lot instead of a fixed start time, an exam prep countdown with no exam date baked in, a weekly study and class planner split into class, study and life instead of an hourly grid, a reading and notes tracker for a one-line takeaway instead of a re-read, a focus-time check-in for noticing when concentration tends to come easier, and an end-of-week reset for what's actually done, what rolls over, and a brain dump before the weekend.

The Big Project Breakdown page is not a new mechanism built just for this planner. It's the same one behind the [ADHD Planner Bundle](/shop/adhd-planner-bundle/)'s [Goal Breakdown page](/shop/adhd-planner-bundle/#p10): a big goal, broken into this month, this week, then one next tiny step small enough to actually start. Here it's pointed at a paper or project instead of a life goal, reused on purpose because it already works, not reinvented for the sake of a new page. If class and study days also need the Bundle's three-chunk daily structure or its no-streak habit tracker, the two planners are built the same way and sit well side by side.
