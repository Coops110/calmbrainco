Most work planners assume a job that runs on a clock: a grid of hours, a meeting that starts exactly when it says it will, a task that takes precisely as long as you guessed. For a lot of ADHD brains, the hard part at work was never the calendar. It's getting a task started in the first place, staying on the one thread you meant to pull, and getting back to it cleanly after something interrupts it. The ADHD Work and Focus Planner is built around that problem instead of around a schedule.

Ten pages: a Workday Top 3 for the real priorities of the day instead of a list of twenty, a Task Breakdown page that takes one big task down to its next tiny step, a Focus Session Tracker with a body-doubling slot and a distraction parking lot, an Email and Message Triage page with exactly three buckets, a Meeting Notes Capture page for decisions made and your own action items, a Context-Switch Reset for the moment something pulls you away from a task, a Waiting-On Tracker so a blocked item doesn't quietly vanish, an End-of-Day Shutdown Ritual for a real stopping point, and a Weekly Work Review with no score attached.

The Task Breakdown page is the same honest mechanism as the [Goal Breakdown page](/shop/adhd-planner-bundle/#p10) in the [ADHD Planner Bundle](/shop/adhd-planner-bundle/), applied to work instead of life goals: a big task, broken into steps, then one next tiny step small enough to actually start.
